# Deeper, Not Bigger
## The $50 AI Influencer Playbook

**Less guessing. More creating.**

You are buying a clearer path: practical instructions, reusable templates, and fewer decisions between an idea and a published post. Software costs are separate. Results depend on the work, the audience, and the offer.

Do these six steps in order. Finish one before starting the next.

## Before you begin

Open these tools:

1. **Writing:** ChatGPT or Claude.
2. **Images:** a generator that accepts a character reference.
3. **Video:** Canva or CapCut.
4. **Tracking:** Google Sheets or Excel.

Create an **AI Creator** folder. Inside, make four folders: **Character**, **Images**, **Videos**, **Results**. Start with one social platform. Check tool costs and commercial-use terms before buying anything.

**Done when:** your folders exist and your tools open.

## 01 — Pick a person and a problem

1. Choose who you help. Example: first-time travelers.
2. Choose one problem. Example: planning an affordable weekend.
3. Search your topic on Instagram or TikTok. Save five relevant accounts.
4. Save three useful posts from each. Note the first sentence and what each post teaches.
5. Write your promise using this template:

> I help [audience] get [benefit] through [repeatable topic or series].

**Example:** “Beautiful weekend escapes for people with ordinary budgets.”

Show the promise to three people in your audience. Ask what they expect you to post. Rewrite it if their answers differ from your goal.

**Done when:** your bio makes the benefit clear in one sentence.

## 02 — Build a consistent character

Paste this into your writing tool:

> Create a character brief for a fictional adult AI creator who helps [audience] with [problem]. Give me a name, a one-line bio that says AI-generated, three personality traits, three weekly content topics, and a consistent visual description. Use [colors and style]. Do not copy a real person or invent personal product experiences.

1. Save the brief in **Character**.
2. Generate an original character in your image tool.
3. Choose one image. Save it as **Master Reference**.
4. Attach it to every new image request. Ask for one scene per image.
5. Make three scenes. Compare the face, hair and distinctive features.

**Image prompt:**

> Use the attached fictional character reference. Keep the same facial features, hair and distinctive marks. Create one vertical scene of the character [action] in [setting]. Use [lighting and colors]. Leave space for a short text overlay. Do not add text, watermarks or extra panels.

Reject blurred detail, changing faces, stray grid lines and awkward crops.

**Done when:** you have a brief, a master reference and three matching scenes.

## 03 — Finish the launch check

- [ ] The bio clearly says the persona is AI-generated.
- [ ] I checked current platform rules and applied required AI labels.
- [ ] I own or have permission to use the assets, voice and likeness.
- [ ] Paid, gifted and affiliate content has clear disclosure.
- [ ] Claims are supportable; the character does not pretend to have used products or attended events.
- [ ] Images pass the quality check and retain provenance metadata.
- [ ] I checked Account Status, eligibility and the profile link.

**Example bio:**

> AI travel muse · budget-friendly weekend ideas  
> One useful escape, every week  
> Get the weekend planner ↓

**Done when:** every box is checked. This is a working checklist, not legal clearance.

## 04 — Make your first reel

A **hook** is the first line that gives someone a reason to watch.

Choose one:

- **Curiosity:** “3 weekend escapes. Which one fits your budget?”
- **Mistake:** “Before you plan your weekend, check these 3 common mistakes.”
- **Utility:** “Your weekend checklist: 5 things to decide first.”

Then build it:

1. Open Canva or CapCut. Create a **1080 × 1920** vertical video.
2. Import your three approved images. Put them in order.
3. Add the hook to the first scene. Start with about two seconds.
4. Add one useful tip per scene. Allow enough time to read it.
5. End with one action: “Get the free checklist through the link in my bio.”
6. Use your own voice or licensed audio. Keep text away from screen edges.
7. Export an MP4. Watch it on your phone. Fix tiny text or wrong facts.
8. Add required labels, publish, and save the post link in **Results**.

**The formula:** promise → useful steps → answer → one next action.

Deliver the promised answer. Verify locations and prices. Label generated scenes.

**Done when:** the video is live and its next-step link works.

## 05 — Give viewers a useful next step

Start with one free resource that matches your posts:

| Your content | Free resource | Possible paid offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekend travel | Packing checklist | Editable itinerary planner |
| Everyday style | Outfit checklist | Capsule wardrobe workbook |
| Creator education | Posting checklist | Content planning workbook |

Build the delivery path:

1. Make a one-page checklist in Canva or a document editor.
2. Export a PDF. Test its links.
3. Create a signup page in an email service such as MailerLite.
4. Explain what people receive and whether they are joining your emails. Include your privacy notice.
5. Put the PDF link in the welcome email or thank-you page.
6. Add the signup URL to your profile if that feature is available.
7. Test the complete process with your own email.

**Done when:** a reader can open your profile, sign up, and receive the resource.

For a paid offer, explain what is included and who it helps. Test checkout before promoting it. Check current eligibility before relying on platform payouts.

**Simple planning math:** monthly revenue goal ÷ price = sales needed, rounded up. At a hypothetical $29 price, a $1,000 gross goal requires 35 sales. At a hypothetical 2% visitor-to-sale rate, that takes 1,750 offer-page visits. These are example inputs, not a forecast. Fees, refunds, taxes and costs reduce net income.

## 06 — Run the 90-day experiment

| When | Do this | If it stalls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–30 | Pin your promise, an example and a next step. Test three recurring video formats. | After ten weak openings, change the first line and first shot. |
| Days 31–60 | Repeat the strongest format. Number episodes. Test the free resource. | Views without follows: clarify the series. Few signups: improve the resource or invitation. |
| Days 61–90 | Test one paid offer. Make a brand sheet with real results. Pitch ten relevant brands. | No sales: ask ten potential buyers about the problem, offer and price before expanding. |

**Every week:** open your analytics. Record views, watch time, shares, new followers, email signups and sales. Pick one strong post. Make a new version with one change.

The interactive edition includes downloadable research and weekly-review CSV worksheets.

## Your next move

**One promise. One reel. One useful next step.**

Write the promise now. You can improve it after you have real feedback.
